
# Assignment 5 — Diffusion Agent Evidence Verification Notes

## 1. Verification purpose

I reviewed selected evidence claims from the primary and contrast test outputs to determine whether the Diffusion Agent used credible, dated sources and accurately distinguished broad AI adoption from application-specific organizational diffusion.

Verification date: October 26, 2026.

## 2. Verified original sources

### Source 1 — McKinsey, State of AI 2025

Original source:
https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025

Publication date: November 5, 2025.

Verified finding:
The survey reported organizational AI use in at least one business function among 88% of respondents. Reported generative AI use was 79%. Approximately one-third had begun scaling AI across their enterprises.

Limitation:
The results reflect surveyed organizational respondents rather than all businesses. Regular use in one function is different from sustained enterprise-wide deployment.

Judgment: Supported with population and measurement qualifications.

### Source 2 — U.S. Census Bureau, Business AI Use

Original source:
https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html

Publication date: May 26, 2026.

Verified finding:
Reported business AI use ranged from approximately 17% to 20% between December 2025 and May 2026. The Information sector reached 39.7%, while Finance and Insurance reached 33.9% in the period ending May 3, 2026.

Limitation:
The survey measures AI use in any business function over the previous two weeks. It does not specifically measure generative AI knowledge assistants. Its population and methodology differ from McKinsey's survey.

Judgment: Supported. Do not average or directly equate these results with McKinsey's percentages.

### Source 3 — Morgan Stanley Internal Assistant

Original source:
https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/ai-at-morgan-stanley-debrief-launch

Publication date: June 26, 2024.

Verified finding:
Morgan Stanley reported that its internal AI Assistant had been fully rolled out in September 2023 and adopted by 98% of financial-advisor teams.

Limitation:
The percentage refers to advisor teams within one institution. It does not establish the percentage of financial-services companies using similar assistants, independent accuracy, or industry-wide institutionalization.

Judgment: Supported as a named organizational deployment example.

### Source 4 — Citi Assist

Original source:
https://www.citigroup.com/global/news/press-release/2025/citi-expands-generative-ai-capabilities-to-australia-and-new-zealand

Publication date: September 17, 2025.

Verified finding:
Citi described Citi Assist as a tool for navigating company policies and procedures. Citi also described broader international rollout of its employee generative AI tools.

Limitation:
Availability and international rollout are not evidence of sustained active use, verified productivity gains, or reliable policy interpretation.

Judgment: Supported as application-specific deployment evidence.

### Source 5 — Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2025

Original source:
https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai

Publication year: 2025.

Verified finding:
84% of respondents were using or planning to use AI development tools, while 51% of professional developers reported daily use. On AI-output accuracy, 46% reported distrust and 33% reported trust.

Limitation:
The 84% figure includes planned adoption and cannot be presented as current use alone. Responses concern participating developers, not representative software-company deployments.

Judgment: Supported, with appropriate denominator and interpretation.

## 3. Claims requiring further verification

Not every citation in the four outputs was independently checked in this verification pass.

Other claims involving the Federal Reserve, Stanford HAI, NIST, the UK financial-services regulatory survey, and specific 2026 Stack Overflow statistics should retain their original-source references and be checked individually before being described as fully verified.

The claim that most financial-services firms or software companies have institutionalized retrieval-grounded internal knowledge assistants remains insufficiently supported by the reviewed sources.

The causal contribution of opinion leaders, change agents, vendor ecosystems, and implementation partners has not been quantified for this particular application.

## 4. Evidence-quality judgment

The strongest evidence supports the conclusion that AI and generative AI have experienced substantial diffusion in several measured populations.

Evidence is substantially weaker for determining the precise Rogers adopter stage of retrieval-grounded internal knowledge assistants within either financial services or software companies.

Survey percentages differ because they measure different populations, geographies, adoption definitions, and observation periods.

Individual deployments demonstrate feasibility and selected organizational adoption, not market-wide maturity.

## 5. AI verification and independent judgment

AI was used to generate and test the specialist outputs, compare cases, and assist with source review. Original publications were consulted to verify the selected figures and organizational deployment claims listed above.

I did not treat a valid JSON response, an AI-generated citation, or a successful deployment announcement as sufficient proof of a diffusion-stage conclusion.

The main limitation is the lack of representative longitudinal data on sustained production use of this exact application among comparable organizations.

## 6. Final implication for the Adoption Agent

Strongest diffusion signal:
Broad reported AI use and identifiable deployments of internal knowledge assistants.

Strongest inhibitor:
The difficulty of producing accurate, authorized, current, and trustworthy answers from internal documentation.

Timing implication:
An aggressive software organization has greater opportunity for earlier bounded learning, while a conservative financial institution requires stronger evidence before broader consequential use.

Caution:
Diffusion evidence alone cannot establish readiness, return on investment, acceptable risk, or the final adoption decision.
