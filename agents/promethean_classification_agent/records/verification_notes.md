## 4. Evidence supporting the preferred classification

Research supports significant architectural advances, flexible cross-task capabilities, and selected research-relevant applications.

It is plausible that these capabilities could support downstream innovation in scientific research and knowledge-intensive economic activity.

The first proposition concerns demonstrated capabilities. The second is a conditional inference about long-term foundational potential.

## 5. Strongest evidence against the preferred classification

Breakthrough is the more directly established classification. Cross-domain technical capabilities do not necessarily produce enduring civilization-scale transformation.

Reliability problems, scientific validation requirements, implementation costs, complementary infrastructure, governance, and human expertise may limit the scope and persistence of benefits.

No reviewed source establishes that generative AI will inevitably restructure civilization.

## 6. Context-specific evidence limitations

**Scientific-research contrast case:** No verified organizational baseline, actual hypothesis-validation outcomes, research productivity improvement, or experimental cost data were supplied.

## 7. Professional judgment and remaining uncertainty

The preferred classification is Potentially Promethean, explicitly qualified. Breakthrough remains the strongest alternative and has stronger direct empirical support.

This distinction must not be used as automatic justification for investment, deployment, increased autonomy, or reduced oversight.

A stronger long-term classification would require independent, reproducible evidence of durable downstream innovation and substantial structural consequences across multiple domains.
