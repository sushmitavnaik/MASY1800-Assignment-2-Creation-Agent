# Assignment 4 — Promethean Classification Agent Test Comparison

## 1. Testing objective

Evaluate whether the agent distinguishes Incremental, Breakthrough, and Potentially Promethean technology significance using explicit evidence, competing classifications, appropriate uncertainty, and context-sensitive implications.

## 2. Test cases

**Primary:** Generative AI using LLMs for an internal knowledge assistant at a hypothetical large financial-services company.

**Contrast:** The same technology for literature synthesis, hypothesis generation, and experimental planning at a hypothetical scientific research organization.

Both tests use a 12-month organizational horizon.

## 3. Initial results — Version 1

Both tests classified generative AI as Potentially Promethean, qualified, while recognizing Breakthrough as the more directly established technological classification.

The primary application was characterized as an incremental improvement in organizational information access. The contrast application was characterized as breakthrough-enabled scientific research assistance with unverified transformative outcomes.

**Observed weakness:** Although the agent identified uncertainty and competing evidence, the justification for preferring Potentially Promethean over Breakthrough relied substantially on broad future possibilities. The evidence threshold distinguishing those classifications needed to be more explicit.

## 4. Instruction revision

Added a higher-significance evidence-calibration and counterfactual comparison requirement.

The revision requires explicit comparison with Breakthrough, separate identification of demonstrated capabilities and speculative outcomes, at least two distinct foundational-impact pathways, relevant enabling conditions, strongest counterevidence, reassessment triggers, and appropriate qualification or abstention.

## 5. Revised results — Version 2

Both revised outputs retained Potentially Promethean as a qualified general classification and explicitly identified Breakthrough as the strongest alternative.

The primary response analyzed pathways in knowledge-intensive work and scientific or technical innovation. It preserved the incremental interpretation of the proposed internal knowledge assistant.

The contrast response examined scientific research and broader knowledge-intensive economic activity, distinguishing idea novelty, experimental feasibility, scientific validation, and enduring institutional effects.

Both outputs preserved the boundary between technological significance and organizational adoption decisions.

## 6. Assessment of improvement

The revised outputs provide more explicit competing-classification analysis, stronger separation between established capabilities and conditional pathways, and more specific counterarguments and future evidence requirements.

The revision improved the clarity and defensibility of the analysis. It did not independently establish that Potentially Promethean is the objectively correct classification.

## 7. Remaining limitations

- Potentially Promethean remains a forward-looking, uncertain judgment.
- The tests used hypothetical organizations without actual performance data.
- Both revised outputs retained the same preferred classification; testing a clearly incremental or ambiguous technology would provide additional evidence of discrimination.
- Individual cited studies must be independently checked before their claims are treated as verified.
- Valid JSON output establishes structural compliance, not factual or analytical correctness.
- The agent must not use technological significance as an automatic justification for organizational adoption.

## 8. Team handoff

**Preferred classification:** Potentially Promethean, qualified.

**Strongest evidence against it:** Demonstrated technical breadth does not yet establish civilization-scale, durable institutional or economic transformation. Breakthrough is more strongly established by current evidence.

**Rule preventing automatic adoption advocacy:** The underlying technology's significance must never substitute for application-level validation, organization-specific evidence, risk assessment, or accountable management judgment.
